# Citadel Sales & Marketing — Action Roadmap

Written 2026-08-15. This is the working plan for building Citadel Sales & Marketing
(trades-focused digital marketing agency, North Idaho) around a full-time 9-to-5 job.
It answers three questions: What do I actually do each night? What do I sell right now
versus later? How do I pitch a service plumber differently than a new-construction excavator?

---

## 1. The three real gaps (diagnosis)

Everything below traces back to three things, not a hundred things:

1. **No proof.** You lost a client to a competitor who had sample work ready to show.
   Right now you have zero demo websites, no documented case study, and your own
   brand isn't finished. Prospects can't picture the result, so they pick whoever
   can show them one.
2. **No time system.** You have ~4-5 hours a night and weekends. Right now that time
   gets absorbed reactively (whatever feels urgent) instead of on a fixed rotation,
   so portfolio-building — the highest-leverage work — keeps losing to whatever's loudest.
3. **Selling things you're not sure you can deliver.** Google Ads and email marketing
   are on your service list but not in your comfort zone. That gap is what's driving
   the fear before outreach calls — you're not scared of the call, you're scared of
   being asked a question about a service you're shaky on.

Fix these three and the rest (SEO depth, channel strategy, confidence) gets much easier,
because you're no longer improvising.

---

## 2. Narrow what you sell right now

You don't need to offer everything on day one. Split your service list into three tiers
and only actively sell Tier 1 and Tier 2 until you've built real reps in Tier 3.

**Tier 1 — Sell now, full confidence**
- Content production: photo, video, drone (you already do this well — it's your actual differentiator, most small trades marketers don't shoot their own footage)
- Google Business Profile setup, optimization, and ongoing management
- Social media management (posting, scheduling, community reply)
- Review generation systems (QR code / text-to-review flow, GBP + Google review funnel)

**Tier 2 — Sell, but scope small and label as "in progress"**
- Website builds (see builder decision below)
- Basic local SEO (NAP consistency, on-page service/city pages, citations)
- Meta/Facebook & Instagram ads (small test budgets, simple objectives — leads or awareness, not full funnels yet)

**Tier 3 — Don't sell solo yet. Either get certified first or white-label it.**
- Google Ads — get Google Skillshop certified (free, ~10-15 hrs total, self-paced:
  search.google.com/skillshop). Until certified, either don't offer it, or pair it
  with a freelance Google Ads specialist you pay a flat fee/rev share to fulfill
  while you own the client relationship.
- Email marketing — same idea. It's a small enough skill to learn in a weekend
  (an email platform's own free course is enough), but don't sell it until you've
  run at least one real sequence, even for yourself.

This isn't about doing less forever — it's about never being on a sales call unsure
whether you can back up what you're pitching. That's what's making outreach feel scary.

## 3. Fix the proof gap — build your "book" (do this in the next 2-3 weeks)

This is the highest-leverage work you can do and it's not client-dependent, so it can't
get bumped by a client emergency. Priority order:

1. **Your own brand is proof #1.** Before anything else, your own website and socials
   need to look like the outcome you're selling. A prospect will check your Instagram
   and your site before they check your pitch deck. Treat "Citadel Sales & Marketing"
   as your first client and give it the full treatment: GBP listing, a real logo,
   consistent color/type across site and socials, a content calendar running on your
   own accounts (even if it's just you talking about trades marketing, job-site content
   tips, before/afters of your own work).

2. **Two demo websites, not generic templates — built for a specific trade persona.**
   Build one for a "service plumber" persona and one for a "new-construction/excavation"
   persona, using real (stock or your own shot) photography, real copy, real service
   pages. Label them clearly as concept/demo work in your portfolio, but make them
   genuinely good — this is what replaces "trust me" with "look at this."
   - Website builder decision: since you want to move up from Durable, evaluate
     **GoHighLevel** vs **Webflow** vs **Duda**. For an agency that wants dashboards,
     review-request automation, and rebillable sub-accounts for clients down the road,
     GoHighLevel is the standard in the local-service marketing niche specifically —
     it bundles the site builder with the GBP/review tooling you're already selling,
     so you're not stitching four tools together. Webflow gives you more design control
     if the visual bar matters more to you than the automation. Pick one this week and
     commit — don't keep shopping builders.

3. **One real case study from your current client.** Even a small social-only package
   has a story: posting cadence, before/after engagement numbers, content examples.
   Document it now as a one-pager (problem → what you did → result, even if the result
   is modest). "Modest but real" beats "hypothetical but big."

4. **A process reel.** 60-90 seconds: you on a job site shooting photo/video/drone,
   a few clips of the output, you talking for 10 seconds about what you do. This proves
   the thing that's actually hardest to fake — that you show up and produce content
   professionally. Post it, but also keep it ready to text/airdrop directly to a prospect.

5. **A one-page leave-behind.** PDF or printed: services, the two demo sites (QR code
   or link), the case study, your contact info. When you get 15 minutes with someone
   from Consolidated Supply, you hand them something physical, not just a conversation.

## 4. Time-blocking system (5-11pm weeknights + weekends)

You don't need more hours, you need the same hours to stop being reactive. Assign each
weeknight a single theme so you're never deciding what to work on when you sit down —
that decision fatigue is a big part of why nights feel like they "go nowhere."

| Night | First 45-60 min (do this before anything else) | Rest of the block |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Outreach: 5 texts/calls to warm contacts, log in tracker | Current client deliverables |
| Tue | Portfolio build (demo site #1 or #2, in rotation) | Same |
| Wed | Outreach follow-ups from Mon + new contacts | Current client deliverables |
| Thu | Your own content: film/edit/schedule Citadel's own socials | Portfolio build |
| Fri | Learning block: Google Ads Skillshop module, or ads platform practice | Admin: invoices, CRM cleanup, next week's plan |
| Sat | Content shoot day if you can get to a job site (yours, a friend's, a favor) | Batch-edit footage for the week |
| Sun | Light/off, or 1-hour review: what moved, what didn't, reset Monday's list | — |

Rules that make this actually stick:
- **Outreach always goes first**, while you still have energy, not last when you're
  depleted. It's the task most likely to get skipped otherwise.
- **One theme per night.** If Tuesday is portfolio night, client emergencies aside,
  outreach doesn't happen Tuesday. This is what stops the "everything, a little, every
  night, nothing finished" pattern.
- Cap nightly work at a real stop time (10:30/11pm). A tired 90-minute block beats a
  drained 3-hour one — you're less likely to burn out and quit in month two.

## 5. Service-based vs. new-construction — different game, different channels

You already have the right instinct here; this makes it concrete. The split that
actually matters isn't "plumber vs. HVAC vs. excavator" — it's **who pays them and how
urgently**: a homeowner with an emergency vs. a GC/developer planning months ahead.

### Service-based trades (residential plumbing/HVAC repair, drain cleaning, small excavation jobs)
Buyer: homeowners, often deciding same-day, comparison-shopping on trust signals.

- **Google Business Profile is the single highest-leverage channel.** "Plumber near me"
  searches convert off the map pack — GBP optimization + review volume/velocity often
  beats anything else you sell them.
- **Review system is not optional** — it directly feeds GBP ranking and is the first
  thing a homeowner checks.
- **Meta ads**: geo-targeted (their service radius), before/after and "we were just at
  this house" content performs well, emergency/urgency messaging ("same-day," "24/7").
- **Nextdoor is genuinely high-value for this segment** — homeowners actively ask
  neighbors "who do you use for X" there, and it's underused by most trades marketers,
  which makes it an edge you can offer.
- **Local SEO**: service + city landing pages ("Coeur d'Alene drain cleaning," "Post
  Falls water heater repair") matter more here than almost anywhere else.
- Google Ads (once you're ready) works well here too — high commercial intent, "near me"
  searches are exactly what Search campaigns are built for.
- LinkedIn: low priority for this segment. Skip it unless the owner wants personal thought-leadership presence.

### New-construction / commercial (custom home plumbing, land development excavation, commercial HVAC)
Buyer: general contractors, developers, sometimes architects — relationship and
credibility-driven, long sales cycles, not impulse decisions.

- **Portfolio-driven website is the anchor asset** — project galleries, timelapses,
  drone site progression, scale and capability signals (equipment, crew size, licenses/bonding).
- **LinkedIn matters here**, specifically — GCs and developers use it, and project-spotlight
  posts (a finished site, a timelapse, a "proud to have partnered with X on Y development")
  build the credibility this buyer is actually evaluating.
- **Referral/relationship content** over paid ads — case studies, GC testimonials,
  association memberships (Home Builders Association chapters, local contractor associations)
  do more here than a Meta ad ever will.
- Directory/association presence and word of mouth outperform paid social for this segment.
  Sponsorship of a local HBA event or trade association can outperform a month of ad spend.
- Paid social/Google ads: lower priority, not zero — mainly useful for brand visibility,
  not lead gen. Don't lead your pitch to this segment with "we'll run your ads."

### YouTube — same answer for both, different job
Not urgent priority #1 for either segment, but valuable for both as a long-term SEO and
trust asset: project walkthroughs and drone footage rank in search and get embedded on
the website/GBP, and double as content you can cut into everything else (Shorts, Reels,
LinkedIn posts). Treat it as a **repurposing target**, not a separate content plan —
film once on the job site, cut it four ways.

**When you're sizing up a new prospect, ask one question first: "Is your customer a
homeowner with a problem today, or a GC planning a project months out?"** That answer
picks the channel mix for you — use the two tables above as the actual menu.

## 6. SEO — concrete checklist, not theory

"SEO" for a local trades business is mostly these five things, roughly in order of
impact per hour invested:

1. **Google Business Profile**: complete every field, weekly GBP posts, respond to
   every review within 48 hours, photos added regularly (this is content you're
   already shooting — reuse it here).
2. **NAP consistency**: Name/Address/Phone identical across GBP, website, Facebook,
   Yelp, Angi, BBB, and any local directories. Inconsistency actively hurts ranking.
3. **On-page service/city pages**: a dedicated page per service per city they serve,
   not one generic "services" page. This is the actual mechanism behind local rankings
   more than backlinks are.
4. **Reviews**: volume and recency both matter. A simple post-job text-to-review link
   is the highest-ROI SEO tool you have.
5. **Backlinks/citations**: for local trades, this means local — chamber of commerce,
   BBB, supplier/manufacturer partner pages (you have real relationships to leverage
   here from your day job), local news sponsorships, HBA membership listing. Skip
   generic backlink schemes; they don't matter for this niche the way local citations do.

A LinkedIn page, a Nextdoor page, a YouTube channel — these aren't "SEO" in the technical
sense, they're **channel decisions**, and section 5 already tells you which client gets which.
Don't set them up by default for every client; set them up when the buyer profile calls for it.

## 7. Outreach — using what you already have

Your actual unfair advantage is five years of real relationships built through
Consolidated Supply. That's not a cold-outreach problem, it's a warm-list problem —
treat it that way.

**Cadence:**
1. Text first, always — low-pressure, doesn't feel like a pitch: *"Hey [name], separate
   from work — I started a marketing company doing content/social/websites for trade
   businesses around North Idaho. Not selling anything over text, just want to grab 15
   min sometime and show you what I'm building. Worth a coffee?"*
2. If they respond with interest, that's when you bring the leave-behind (section 3.5)
   and the phone with the two demo sites pulled up — show, don't describe.
3. Log every contact in a simple tracker (name, business, date texted, response,
   follow-up date) — right now this is living in your head, which is part of why it
   feels chaotic. A basic spreadsheet with a "next follow-up" column removes the guesswork.
4. Follow up on a fixed schedule (e.g., day 3, day 10, day 30) instead of "whenever
   you remember to." Most sales are lost to no-follow-up, not to a bad pitch.

You don't need a cold-call script. You need the leave-behind finished (section 3) so the
warm relationship has something concrete to look at — that's what turns "yeah let's grab
coffee sometime" into an actual client.

## 8. 30-day sequence

**Week 1**: Finish Citadel's own brand (site + GBP + one social platform posting
consistently). Pick the website builder and commit. Start the tracker spreadsheet.

**Week 2**: Build demo website #1 (service-trade persona). Shoot/edit the process reel.
Text 10 warm contacts using the outreach script.

**Week 3**: Build demo website #2 (new-construction persona). Finish the one-page
leave-behind. Document the current client case study. Continue outreach follow-ups.

**Week 4**: Start Google Ads Skillshop certification (Fri learning blocks). Review what
worked in outreach, adjust the script. Book at least 2 in-person/call meetings using the
finished leave-behind and demo sites.

By day 30 you should have: a finished personal brand, two demo sites, one case study,
one process reel, one leave-behind, a live contact tracker, and at least a handful of
warm meetings booked using actual proof instead of a pitch alone.
